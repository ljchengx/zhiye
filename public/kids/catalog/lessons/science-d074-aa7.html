Why Do Fireflies Glow?

Have you ever seen tiny lights floating in the night

sky?

They look like little stars!

Those are fireflies.

But why do they glow?

Let's find out!

Fireflies have a special glow organ in their abdomen.

Inside this organ, there are two important things:

luciferin and luciferase.

When oxygen enters the ___.

organ, luciferase helps luciferin react with oxygen.

This creates light!

The light is very efficient—almost no heat is given off, so it does not burn the firefly.

That is why it is called "cold light."

Fireflies glow for different reasons.

The main reason is to find a mate.

Male fireflies flash their lights in a special pattern to say, "I am here!" Female fireflies

flash back to say, "Come find me!" Each kind of

Abdomen (Glow Organ) Luciferin + Oxygen

firefly has its own special flash pattern.

Fireflies also glow to stay safe.

Their light warns

predators: "Do not eat me!

I taste bad!" Some fireflies even have poisonous blood, so other animalslearn to stay away.

Even firefly eggs and larvae can glow!

They use

their light to scare away enemies too.

Fireflies are amazing!

They can make their own light without getting hot.

They use it to talk, find love, and protect themselves.

Firefly light is almost 100%efficient-

that means almost all the energy

becomes light, not heat!
