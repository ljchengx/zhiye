Dumplings: A Taste of Chinese Tradition

Read and Learn Dumplings: A Taste of Chinese Tradition Dumplings, called jiaozi in Chinese, are one of the most famous foods in China.

Legend says that a wise doctor named Zhang Zhongjing invented them long ago.

传说一位叫张忠京的明智医生,早就发明了这些药.

He made small dough wrappers filled with meat and herbs to warm people's ears in winter.

他做了小钱包 装满肉和草药 在冬天用来温暖人们的耳朵。

That is why dumplings are also called "ear-protecting" food.

因此饺子也被称为"保护耳朵"食物.

A dumpling looks like an ancient Chinese silver ingot, which means wealth and good luck.

饺子看起来像中国古代的银器,意思是财富和好运.

People often say, "If you eat dumplings, you will have a lucky year." Making dumplings is a fun family activity.

人们常说:"如果吃饺子,你会有一个幸运的一年",制作饺子是一种有趣的家庭活动.

First, you mix flour with water to make dough.

首先,你用面粉和水搅拌,赚钱。

Then you roll the dough into thin circles.

然后把钱卷成薄圆形

Next, you put a spoon of filling (usually pork, cabbage, or chives) in the center.

接下来,在中央放一勺填料(通常是猪肉,小白菜,或辣椒).

Finally, you fold the edges and pinch them together.

最后,你折叠的边缘 并捏在一起。

It takes practice to make a perfect shape!

做一个完美的形状需要练习!

In China, families usually eat dumplings during the Spring Festival, especially at midnight on NewYear's Eve.

在中国,家庭通常在春节期间吃饺子,尤其是在新年除夕的午夜.

They also eat dumplings on the Winter Solstice to keep warm.

他们还在冬溶液上食用饺子以保持温暖.

Some people hide a coin inside one dumpling.

有人把硬币藏在一个饺子里

Whoever finds it will have good fortune in the new year.

找到它的人在新年就会有好运

Dumplings are not just food—they are a symbol of family togetherness and happiness.

饺子不仅仅是食物,而是家庭团结和幸福的象征。
