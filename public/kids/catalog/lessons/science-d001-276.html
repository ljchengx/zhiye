The Bronze Mask with Protruding Eyes

Tell China’s Stories in English

The Bronze Mask

with Protruding Eyes

商铜纵目面具

TheBronzeMaskwithProtrudingEyesisabronze artifact from the Middle and Late Shang Dynasty (1600-1046 BC).

Itwas unearthed in 1986 from Sacrificial Pit No.2at theSanxingdui Site in Sichuan Province.

It is one of the largest bronze masks unearthed at Sanxingdui and is now preserved in the Sanxingdui Museum.

The mask has amazing features.

Its eyebrows are sharp and upturned.

The eyes are long and slanted,

andthe eyeballsareextremelyexaggerated

cylindrical and protruding forward upto16 centimetres.

The ears stretch widely to both sides.

It has a short nose bridge, with nostrils shaped like an ox's nose and curling upward.

The mouth is wide and deep, with long, upturned corners, as if revealing the tip of its tongue in a mysterious smile.

Because the most striking parts of this large bronze mask are its protruding eyes and widespread ears, people call it the “Eyes That See a Thousand Miles" and “Ears That Catch the Wind".

Such an exaggerated design shows that it has superhuman powers.

Combined with records about “Cancong with Protruding Eyes", this mask may be the divine image of Cancong, the first king of the ancient Shu Kingdom.

中文翻译: 商铜纵目面具

商铜纵目面具为商代中晚期青铜器，该面具于1986年出土于三星堆遗址二号祭祀坑，是三星堆出土青铜器中形体最大的两件面具之一，现收藏于三星堆博物馆。

其形象特征为：眉尖上挑，双眼斜长，眼球呈极度夸张的柱状，向前凸伸长达16厘米；双耳向两侧充分展开；短鼻梁，鼻翼呈牛鼻状向上内卷；口阔而深，唇缝深长上扬，似微露舌尖，作神秘微笑状。

因为大型青铜纵目面具最为夸张的是凸出的双眼和舒展的大耳，因此有人称之为“千里眼”“顺风耳”。

夸张的造型表明它具有超人的神通。

结合有关“蚕丛纵目”的记载推断，此像可能是第一代蜀王蚕丛的神像。
